# Slide Patterns

Use this library to choose slides. A main deck should not include every pattern.

## Core Slide Types

| Slide Type | Purpose | Best Placement | Strong Evidence | Layout Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | Establish category, tone, and one-line promise | First | Company name, short positioning line, date/round | Minimal cover, product/customer image, or clean brand field |
| Snapshot | Give investors quick context | Early, often slide 2 for later stage | Key metrics, customers, round, geography, stage | KPI strip + concise company description |
| Traction Teaser | Earn attention fast | Slide 2 for Series A+ | Revenue, growth, retention, customers, usage, pipeline | 3-5 metric tiles or one hero growth chart |
| Market Shift | Explain why now | Early before problem or after proof | Regulation, platform shift, macro pressure, buyer behavior | Timeline, before/after, external signals |
| Problem | Create urgency | Early | Customer pain, wasted spend/time, failed process, risk | One stark headline + 2-3 quantified pain points |
| Broken Alternatives | Show why status quo fails | After problem | Incumbents, manual process, spreadsheets, substitutes | Comparison row, "today vs needed", or failure map |
| Solution | Make the product feel inevitable | After problem/alternatives | Product promise, workflow, demo visual | Hero product visual + 3-part value proposition |
| Product Workflow | Show how it works | After solution | Screens, steps, integrations, architecture | Step-by-step flow or annotated product strip |
| Customer Outcome | Prove value | After product or before traction detail | Case study, ROI, time saved, revenue lift, testimonial | Before/after, case card, or outcome table |
| Traction Detail | Prove the business works | Early/mid for Series A+ | Revenue, usage, logos, cohort, retention, NRR | Growth chart with takeaway headline |
| Unit Economics | Prove quality of growth | Mid after traction | Margin, CAC, payback, LTV/CAC, contribution | Waterfall, margin stack, or simple equation |
| GTM Motion | Show repeatability | After traction/economics | ICP, channels, sales cycle, pipeline, partners | Funnel, channel map, ICP target, or motion diagram |
| Market Size | Prove venture scale | Mid after wedge is clear | TAM/SAM/SOM, bottoms-up model, expansion segments | Concentric circles, layered bars, or wedge-to-market map |
| Competition | Clarify category and differentiation | After product and market context | Competitor map, feature gap, customer reason to switch | 2x2, matrix, or "why we win" table |
| Moat | Show durability | After competition or GTM | Data, network, regulatory, product depth, distribution, cost | Flywheel, stack, compounding loop, defensibility ladder |
| Roadmap/Vision | Expand belief beyond wedge | Late | Product expansion, geography, segments, platform path | Timeline, horizon map, product ladder |
| Team | Prove ability to execute | Late unless team is the core reason | Relevant founder history, domain expertise, hiring bench | Headshots + relevance bullets, not generic bios |
| Financial Plan | Show path and ambition | Late or appendix depending detail | Revenue plan, margin, burn, milestones | 3-5 year chart set with clear assumptions |
| Round/Use Of Funds | Convert belief into action | Last main-deck slide | Raise amount, allocation, milestones, runway | Capital allocation + milestone table |
| Appendix Divider | Keep diligence modular | After main deck | Section label | Minimal divider |

## Sequencing Heuristics

- Use "traction teaser -> problem -> solution" when proof is the strongest hook.
- Use "market shift -> problem -> solution" when the category is emerging or unfamiliar.
- Use "problem -> alternatives -> solution" when pain is obvious but differentiation needs setup.
- Use "technical bottleneck -> platform -> validation" when the hard thing is scientific, technical, or regulated.
- Use "current state -> proof -> why now -> milestones" for extensions and bridges.
- Use "scale proof -> market -> repeatability -> expansion" for growth-stage decks.

## Appendix Candidates

Move these out of the main deck unless they are central to the investment thesis:

- Detailed financial model or cohort tables.
- Full product screenshots that require zooming.
- Customer reference pages beyond 1-2 main proof slides.
- Technical architecture beyond a simplified platform diagram.
- Regulatory details, licenses, patents, clinical evidence, or compliance workflows.
- Board/advisor pages unless a specific person materially changes credibility.
- Pipeline lists, market-by-market plans, long implementation details.

## Common Repairs

- If a deck feels slow: move traction or the sharpest market shift into slide 2.
- If a deck feels vague: rewrite each title as a claim, not a topic label.
- If a deck feels crowded: split proof from explanation; one slide should not teach market, product, and business model at once.
- If a deck feels like a product tour: insert customer pain and outcomes before deeper feature slides.
- If a deck feels like a consulting report: reduce labels and bullets, increase takeaway headlines and proof objects.
- If a deck feels risky: add a slide that directly addresses the biggest investor objection.
