# Narrative Arcs

Use these arcs as starting points. Choose one, then adapt to the user's company and investor audience.

## Seed: Insight To Wedge

Purpose: create belief before the company has deep financial proof.

Sequence:
1. Name the sharp insight or behavior shift.
2. Show the customer pain and why existing options fail.
3. Reveal the product as the obvious response.
4. Show early evidence that real users/customers care.
5. Explain the wedge, business model, and expansion path.
6. Show why the team is unusually suited.
7. Ask for capital tied to the next validation milestone.

Use when:
- The product/category is novel.
- Traction is early but customer pain is clear.
- Founder-market fit or technical insight carries weight.

Avoid:
- Over-indexing on market-size slides before the investor believes the wedge.
- Hiding weak traction behind broad category claims.

## Series A: Proof To Scale

Purpose: prove the company is working and can become much larger with repeatable growth.

Sequence:
1. Start with the strongest traction proof.
2. Explain the problem and why customers switch now.
3. Show the product/workflow and customer outcomes.
4. Quantify repeatability: growth, retention, unit economics, payback, pipeline, or usage.
5. Define the focused ICP and market expansion path.
6. Establish competitive durability.
7. Show the team and operating system.
8. Tie the round to milestones that justify the next financing.

Use when:
- Revenue, usage, customer wins, retention, or pipeline are strong enough to lead.
- The investor's main question is whether traction can scale.

Avoid:
- Spending too many early slides on generic problem framing.
- Treating the round slide as administrative; it should complete the investment logic.

## Extension Or Bridge: Milestone Unlock

Purpose: explain why incremental capital now gets the company to a clear next valuation or financing state.

Sequence:
1. Re-anchor current business status.
2. Show what has improved since the prior round.
3. Highlight the few metrics that prove the base business works.
4. Name the market, product, or GTM catalyst.
5. Explain the focused plan.
6. Show exactly what the round funds and what milestones it unlocks.

Use when:
- The round is smaller than a full priced round.
- The company has proof but needs time/capital to reach a stronger next round.

Avoid:
- Rewriting the whole company origin story.
- Leaving milestones vague.

## Growth Or Series B+: Scale Dominance

Purpose: show a large, de-risked company compounding toward category leadership.

Sequence:
1. Start with scale proof: revenue, volume, customer base, network, geography, or operational footprint.
2. Show the market category and why it is expanding.
3. Prove repeatable acquisition and retention.
4. Show margin, operating leverage, or capital efficiency.
5. Explain expansion vectors and strategic moat.
6. Detail capital use against acceleration, not survival.

Use when:
- Investors already understand the category.
- The company has enough proof to make scale the story.

Avoid:
- Seed-style education unless the market is still misunderstood.
- Dense operating detail in the main deck.

## Technical Or Frontier: De-Risk The Hard Thing

Purpose: show that a technical breakthrough maps to a large commercial outcome.

Sequence:
1. Explain the unmet market or scientific/technical bottleneck.
2. Show why current approaches cannot solve it.
3. Introduce the platform architecture in one simplified diagram.
4. Decompose the hard thing into proof milestones.
5. Show validation: experiments, partnerships, IP, regulatory progress, pilots, or manufacturing feasibility.
6. Connect to business model, initial use case, and expansion.
7. Ask for capital tied to the next de-risking milestones.

Use when:
- The investor needs to believe a hard product can work.
- The company is biotech, deep tech, hardware, infrastructure, data, AI, or regulated.

Avoid:
- Letting technical architecture replace commercial urgency.
- Putting all science/engineering detail in the main deck; use appendix.

## Marketplace Or Network: Liquidity Flywheel

Purpose: prove the company can solve a coordination problem and compound through supply, demand, or data.

Sequence:
1. Show the broken market or inefficient workflow.
2. Identify the constrained side and why it has not been solved.
3. Show the product mechanism that creates liquidity or data advantage.
4. Prove one side is reachable and the other side benefits.
5. Show early flywheel evidence: repeat behavior, density, transaction growth, revenue, or improved outcomes.
6. Explain monetization and expansion.

Use when:
- The business depends on supply/demand density, payments, data sharing, or ecosystem coordination.

Avoid:
- Claiming network effects before showing the initial loop.

## Regulated Or Operational: Trust And Execution

Purpose: prove the company can win in a complex market where execution, compliance, and operations are the moat.

Sequence:
1. Show the market pain and regulatory/operational barrier.
2. Explain why incumbents or DIY approaches are costly or slow.
3. Show the integrated solution: software, operations, compliance, payments, logistics, or service layer.
4. Prove customer trust and operational performance.
5. Show margins, coverage, capacity, or service quality improving with scale.
6. Explain expansion playbook and capital use.

Use when:
- The moat is not only software; it includes licenses, operations, data, supplier access, or regulated workflows.

Avoid:
- Apologizing for operational complexity; show how it becomes advantage.
